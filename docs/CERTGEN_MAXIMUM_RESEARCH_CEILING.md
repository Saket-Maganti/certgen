# CertGen Maximum Research Ceiling

## Direct verdict

CertGen's maximum credible identity on the current code is a **prospective generative-model evaluation methodology and audit**, with reproducibility/evidence integrity as the secondary contribution. It is not a new general confidence-sequence theory, a new metric, or a metric-agnostic ranking framework.

The central scientific question is worth testing: how many apparent generative-model improvements remain directionally unresolved at common budgets under a continuously monitored, prospectively locked protocol? The repository currently supplies no empirical answer.

## Why the ceiling is narrower than the historical pitch

Established work already provides MMD/KID evaluation, bounded-mean confidence sequences, sequential kernel tests, e-values/e-BH, and benchmark-uncertainty critiques. Gao, Sun, and Su (2025) directly address uncertainty-quantified relative generative-model comparison on real image/text tasks. CertGen's plausible delta is the combination of sample-only bounded-kernel access, time-uniform optional stopping, prospective multiplicity, right-censored decision time, partial rankings, and immutable evidence gates.

That combination becomes a scientific contribution only if a real prospective audit reveals a consequential result. The software protocol alone is not a main-track paper.

## Ceiling tiers

| Tier | Evidence | Credible output | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current | conditional method plus tests; no real inputs | audited research software scaffold | no submission |
| First pilot | one complete 1k CIFAR lineage and controls | technical pilot / internal report | not paper-ready |
| Minimum study | two image families, prospectively selected systems, controls, full censoring and sensitivity | bounded-kernel evaluation methodology paper | AISTATS/WACV/TMLR plausible depending on result |
| Strong study | three meaningful families or one theory-valid modern extension, closest baselines, independent reproduction, consequential rankings | strong evaluation/benchmark paper | evaluation track or high-risk ICLR/main-track attempt |
| Maximum | strong study plus material inferential/theory advance and systematic literature/artifact audit | combined method-and-audit contribution | NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR/CVPR main remains possible but high risk |

These are fit assessments, not acceptance predictions.

## Results that would justify the paper

- a nontrivial registered fraction of contestable pairs remains unresolved at realistic budgets;
- early valid decisions substantially reduce realized online sample/resource use;
- a certified partial order differs materially from common point-estimate rankings;
- protocol choices change edge resolution in a prospectively controlled way; or
- the audit uncovers a reproducible reporting/artifact gap with practical evaluation consequences.

The opposite outcomes are still publishable facts only if the protocol is credible. If almost every pair decides, the uncertainty headline fails; the paper must pivot to the observed early-decision or reproducibility result without searching post hoc for harder pairs.

## Required theory for a higher ceiling

- an established, mapped, tested alternative to the conservative union-Hoeffding route;
- continuum-valid betting-CS inversion if that method returns;
- a conditional/shared-prompt estimand and stream for text-to-image;
- directional e-values and valid false-discovery/ranking control under the actual global stopping filtration; or
- a proved finite-population route if unique without-replacement reference traversal is desired.

Until then, FID/FD-DINOv2 and polynomial KID remain descriptive and e-BH remains exploratory fixed-time point-null analysis.

## Recommended venue strategy

Do not choose a venue before the pilot and minimum-study outcome. The realistic robust target for a rigorous full methodology study is TMLR; AISTATS or WACV may fit a focused minimum version. Evaluation/data tracks fit a broad audit. A main-track attempt requires maximum breadth, consequence, and closest-work differentiation.
