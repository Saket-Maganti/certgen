\paragraph{Generative-evaluation metrics.}
Fr\'echet Inception Distance (Heusel et al., 2017) and Inception Score (Salimans
et al., 2016) remain the default summaries of generative quality, despite known
estimator bias: FID is biased at finite sample sizes and sensitive to the
extrapolation to infinite samples (Chong and Forsyth, 2020). Kernel Inception
Distance (Bi\'nkowski et al., 2018) replaces the Fr\'echet form with an unbiased
MMD estimate; CMMD (Jayasumana et al., 2024) argues for CLIP embeddings with an
unbiased MMD; and FD-DINOv2 (Stein et al., 2023) studies an alternative feature
space. CertGen is deliberately \emph{not} a new metric: it is a decision layer
over a compatible statistic. We keep Fr\'echet-form scores descriptive because
this repository contains no audited bounded-mean stream for them. The current
claim-capable route is limited to the implemented bounded RBF-MMD difference
stream; other metric names do not inherit that guarantee.

\paragraph{Evaluation reliability and reproducibility.}
Reported numbers are fragile to preprocessing: aliased resizing and other
pipeline subtleties materially change FID (Parmar et al., 2022). This motivates
our preprocessing lock and the requirement that a reported point estimate be
reproduced within tolerance \emph{before} any certificate is trusted. A separate
line studies benchmark and ranking reliability, largely in NLP (e.g., Dror et
al., 2018, on significance testing); generative-vision leaderboards have received
comparatively little such scrutiny, which is the gap CertGen targets.

\paragraph{Anytime-valid inference and e-processes.}
Our statistical machinery is borrowed, and we say so plainly. Time-uniform
confidence sequences (Howard et al., 2021), betting-based confidence sequences
for bounded means (Waudby-Smith and Ramdas, 2023), and the broader theory of
e-values, test (super)martingales, and e-processes (Vovk and Wang, 2021; Ramdas
et al., 2023) provide validity under optional stopping when their assumptions
are met. e-BH (Wang and Ramdas, 2022) controls false discovery rate under
arbitrary dependence for valid e-values. CertGen currently treats its e-BH path
as exploratory: its terminal point-null e-values do not by themselves certify a
direction, and adaptive stopping across a family still requires a matched proof.
The conservative primary family protocol is Bonferroni correction.

\paragraph{Sequential and kernel two-sample testing (closest prior work).}
The most direct neighbors are sequential two-sample and kernel tests:
betting/e-value two-sample and independence testing (e.g., Shekhar and Ramdas,
2024; Podkopaev et al., 2023) instantiate exactly the kind of anytime-valid
kernel comparison we rely on. We claim no methodological novelty over this line.
CertGen's intended contribution is orthogonal and applied: (i) framing generative-model
\emph{leaderboard wins} as a set of simultaneous, optional-stopping-safe
decisions on the paired discrepancy $\Delta_{AB}=d(A,R)-d(B,R)$; (ii) a
prospectively multiplicity-controlled \emph{audit} of how many reported wins are statistically decided
at the sample sizes actually used; and (iii) a reproducibility discipline
(provenance ledger, preprocessing lock, reproduction gate) that makes such an
audit defensible on released samples and feature caches. In short, prior work
gives the inferential machinery; CertGen proposes to test, on prospectively
registered real benchmarks, whether the field's comparisons survive it. That
empirical question remains unanswered in the current repository.

\paragraph{Statistical inference for generative-model comparison.}
Gao, Sun, and Su (2025) directly study uncertainty-quantified relative
generative-model performance using a relative KL score, including image and
text experiments and a conditional-model extension. This is the closest work to
CertGen's scientific question. Their fixed-sample route relies on accessible
model densities or approximations; CertGen's remaining proposed delta is a
sample-only bounded-kernel estimand with time-uniform monitoring, prospectively
controlled comparison families, censored samples-to-decision, and partial-order
audit outputs. These differences require direct theoretical and empirical
comparison and are not yet validated here.
